Expression-tree evolution after Karl Sims, 1991
Click any image: it moves to the centre and the other eight become its mutants. Click the centre to mutate again. ⤢ opens a large view. ♥ two images to mate them.
Evolves a population to minimise distance to the target (multi-scale RMS colour error, after a best-fit colour scale per channel). The population is split into islands that evolve separately and swap champions in a ring; every few generations the two most different-looking of the islands' top matches are mated, and a child is kept only if it beats both parents. The grid shows the overall best in the centre and each island's champion (★) around it — ⚭ marks a child of diverse mating, → a migrant. ♥ two to mate them yourself; click one to take it to interactive breeding.

Match weights

Each measure is scored relative to a flat image of the target's average colour (0 = perfect, 1 = no better than flat); the match error is their weighted mean. Changing weights re-scores the whole population.

Switch off whole families or single functions (▸). Applies to new random images, mutations and auto-evolve; existing genomes keep what they already contain.
Lets each ◎ function (rings, spirals, stars, rotate, zoom, swirl, kaleidoscope, fractals…) have its own centre instead of the middle of the image. The slider sets how often a new function gets an offset; mutation also moves, adds and removes centres. In genomes an offset is written after the name: (rings@0.3,-0.2 5).
Runs a function only inside a box with smoothed edges: inside you see the function, outside its first argument passes through unchanged, so features stay local. Only functions with arguments get boxes (a boxed leaf typed by hand fades to mid-grey). The slider sets how often a new function gets a box; mutation moves, resizes, softens, adds and removes boxes. Written (swirl#cx,cy,w,h,soft 0.7 …), with w and h the half-width and half-height.
